# Precipitation intensity and accumulation

Precipitation is reported two ways, and they answer different questions.

- **Intensity** is a rate: how hard it is falling, in millimetres per hour.
- **Accumulation** is a total: how much fell over a period, in millimetres.

One millimetre of accumulation is one litre of water per square metre.

## Intensity classes

Conventional descriptive bands for rain, given as a guide:

| Description | Rate |
|---|---|
| Light | up to about 2.5 mm/h |
| Moderate | about 2.5 to 10 mm/h |
| Heavy | about 10 to 50 mm/h |
| Violent | above about 50 mm/h |

Snow is measured as the *water equivalent* after melting, which is why a snowy day can show a
small precipitation total. Depending on how wet the snow is, 1 mm of water equivalent can produce
anywhere from about 5 mm to 20 mm of settled depth.

## Why the distinction matters

The same daily total means very different things depending on its intensity. Ten millimetres spread
evenly across a day soaks gently into soil. The same ten millimetres in twenty minutes runs off
hard surfaces, overwhelms drains, and causes surface flooding. Accumulation alone cannot tell you
which happened, which is why an hourly series is more informative than a daily total when the
question is about impact.

## Reading a total honestly

A daily accumulation is a sum over a defined window, and the window matters: totals over a local
calendar day and totals over a 24-hour period from a fixed hour are different numbers. Weathra
sums over the *location's own* calendar day, so "Tuesday's rainfall" means Tuesday where the
weather happened.

A total of zero and a total that is unavailable are also different facts. Weathra reports an absent
measure as absent rather than as zero.
