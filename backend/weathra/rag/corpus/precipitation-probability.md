# Precipitation probability

Precipitation probability is the chance that measurable precipitation — conventionally at least
0.1 mm or 0.2 mm depending on the service — falls at a given point within a given period.

## What the number actually means

"A 40% chance of rain tomorrow" means that in a large number of days with atmospheric conditions
like tomorrow's, measurable rain fell on about 40 of every 100. It is a statement about a
population of similar situations, not about one day.

Two things it does **not** mean:

- **It is not the fraction of the area that will get wet**, although the two are related in
  showery conditions and are sometimes conflated.
- **It is not the fraction of the time it will rain.** A 40% chance is not "raining 40% of the
  day".

The period and the point matter enormously. A 30% chance over an hour and a 30% chance over a day
describe very different situations, and a probability quoted for a region is not the probability
for one street in it.

## Probability and amount are independent

A high probability with a small amount — near-certain drizzle — and a low probability with a large
amount — an unlikely but heavy thunderstorm — are both ordinary forecasts. Neither number can be
derived from the other, which is why they are reported separately and why an amount must never be
turned into a probability or the reverse.

## Where Weathra gets it

Weathra reports precipitation probability only when the weather provider supplies it. Some data
sources — reanalysis archives of what actually happened, in particular — carry no probability at
all, because a past day either had rain or did not. In that case Weathra reports the probability as
unavailable rather than inferring one from the recorded amount.
